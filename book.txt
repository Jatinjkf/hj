1. INTRODUCTION TO GENERATIVE AI
================================================================================

Generative AI refers to artificial intelligence systems capable of generating text,
images, or other media in response to prompts. Stable Diffusion is a specific type
of generative AI known as a "Latent Diffusion Model" (LDM). Unlike earlier AI art
generators that simply mashed images together, diffusion models learn the underlying
patterns of visual data.

Imagine teaching a computer what a cat looks like not by showing it a cat, but by
showing it a photo of a cat and slowly destroying it with static (noise) until it
is unrecognizable. Then, you ask the computer to reverse the process: start with
static and try to find the cat. If you do this millions of times with millions of
images, the computer eventually learns how to "hallucinate" a cat out of pure
random noise. This is the essence of diffusion.

================================================================================
2. HOW STABLE DIFFUSION WORKS
================================================================================

To master AI generation, you don't need a PhD, but understanding the mechanism
helps you control the output.

### The Forward Process (Training)
During training, the model takes a clear image and adds Gaussian noise (static)
to it step-by-step.
Step 0: Clear Photo
Step 10: Slightly Grainy
Step 100: Very Fuzzy
Step 1000: Pure Noise

The model's job is to predict the noise that was added at each step. By learning
to predict the noise, it implicitly learns the image structure.

### The Reverse Process (Generation)
When you generate an image, the process runs backward.
1. The system creates a canvas of pure random noise (based on your Seed).
2. The Text Encoder converts your prompt into vectors (numbers) the model understands.
3. The U-Net (the brain) looks at the noise and your prompt and predicts:
   "Given this prompt, what part of this static looks like noise?"
4. The Scheduler subtracts that predicted noise.
5. This repeats for a set number of "Steps".
6. The result is a clear image representation in "Latent Space".

### The Latent Space
Working with full-resolution images (e.g., 512x512 pixels) requires massive
computation. Stable Diffusion cheats by working in "Latent Space". It compresses
the image by a factor of 8 (e.g., to 64x64). This compressed version contains all
the semantic information but is much smaller and faster to process.

================================================================================
3. CORE COMPONENTS
================================================================================

A Stable Diffusion system isn't just one model; it's a pipeline of several.

### 1. The Text Encoder (CLIP)
This is the eyes/ears of the system. It translates human language (your prompt)
into mathematical embeddings. If CLIP doesn't understand a concept (e.g., a specific
celebrity or obscure object), the U-Net won't be able to generate it.
*   **Tip:** CLIP works best with descriptive, comma-separated tokens rather than
    complex grammatical sentences.

### 2. The U-Net
The heavy lifter. It takes the noisy latent image and the text embeddings and
predicts the noise residue. It is responsible for the actual "drawing".
*   **Optimization:** This is the part usually optimized by OpenVINO or TensorRT.

### 3. The VAE (Variational Autoencoder)
The translator.
-   **Encoder:** Compresses a real image into Latent Space (used in Img2Img).
-   **Decoder:** Expands the final Latent result back into a viewable Pixel Image.
*   **Tip:** A bad VAE can result in washed-out colors. Many models bake in a good
    VAE, but sometimes you need to load a separate one (e.g., `vae-ft-mse-840000`).

### 4. The Scheduler (Sampler)
The strategist. It decides how much noise to remove at each step.
Different schedulers have different "personalities":
-   **Euler a (Ancestral):** Fast, chaotic. The image changes significantly if you
    add more steps. Good for creative exploration.
-   **Euler:** Standard, reliable.
-   **DPM++ 2M Karras:** The gold standard for speed and quality. Converges fast
    (15-20 steps) and produces sharp details.
-   **DDIM:** Deterministic, good for inpainting.
-   **Heun:** High quality but slow (takes 2 steps for every 1 logical step).

4. KEY PARAMETERS & HOW TO USE THEM
================================================================================

### Prompt
What you want to see.
*   **Format:** `Subject, Action, Context, Art Style, Artist, Technical Specs`
*   **Example:** `A cute cat, sitting on a fence, sunny day, oil painting, by Van Gogh, 8k resolution`

### Negative Prompt
What you **don't** want to see. This is as important as the positive prompt.
*   **Usage:** Use it to remove defects or styles.
*   **Standard Negatives:** `ugly, deformed, bad anatomy, blurry, watermark, text, low quality`

### Seed
The DNA of the image. The seed determines the initial pattern of random noise.
*   **Same Seed + Same Settings = Same Image.**
*   **Random Seed (-1):** Good for exploration.
*   **Fixed Seed:** Good for tweaking. If you like an image but want to change the
    color, keep the seed fixed and change the prompt slightly.

### Steps
How many times the denoising process runs.
*   **Low (1-10):** Unfinished, messy, abstract.
*   **Medium (15-25):** The sweet spot for modern samplers (DPM++).
*   **High (30-50):** Diminishing returns. Adds fine detail but takes longer.
*   **Very High (100+):** Usually unnecessary and can "burn" the image (too much contrast).

### CFG Scale (Classifier Free Guidance)
How strictly the model follows your prompt.
*   **Low (1-6):** Creative, artistic, might ignore prompt details.
*   **Medium (7-12):** The standard. Balanced adherence to prompt and image quality.
*   **High (13-20):** Very strict, but can cause "frying" (artifacts, high contrast, weird colors).
*   **Recommended:** 7.0 - 8.0.

### Dimensions (Width/Height)
*   **SD 1.5 Standard:** 512x512.
*   **Portrait:** 512x768.
*   **Landscape:** 768x512.
*   **Warning:** Generating non-standard sizes (e.g., 1024x1024) on SD 1.5 often
    results in "duplication" (two heads, repeating bodies) because the model wasn't
    trained on that size. Use "Hires Fix" (generate small, upscale later) instead.

### Batch Size & Batch Count
*   **Batch Size:** Number of images generated *in parallel*. Uses more VRAM/RAM.
*   **Batch Count:** Number of images generated *sequentially*. Takes more time.
*   **Recommendation:** On iGPUs/CPUs, keep Batch Size = 1 to save memory. Increase
    Batch Count to get multiple variations.

================================================================================
5. HARDWARE & OPTIMIZATION
================================================================================

### GPU vs CPU
Stable Diffusion runs best on NVIDIA GPUs (CUDA). However, with optimizations like
OpenVINO, it can run surprisingly well on Intel CPUs and iGPUs.
*   **OpenVINO:** Converts the model into an "Intermediate Representation" (IR)
    that runs efficiently on Intel hardware. It fuses operations and manages memory better.

### FP16 vs FP32
*   **FP32 (Full Precision):** High accuracy, high memory usage. Default for CPUs.
*   **FP16 (Half Precision):** Slightly lower accuracy (negligible for art), half
    the memory usage, much faster. Default for GPUs.
*   **OpenVINO Optimization:** Often quantizes weights to FP16 automatically for GPUs.

### RAM vs VRAM
*   **VRAM (Video RAM):** Dedicated GPU memory. SD 1.5 needs ~4GB minimum for comfort.
*   **RAM (System Memory):** Used by CPU and iGPUs (shared memory). If you use an
    iGPU, your system RAM speed (DDR4 vs DDR5) significantly impacts performance.

6. ADVANCED TECHNIQUES
================================================================================

### Image-to-Image (Img2Img)
Instead of starting with random noise, start with an existing image + some noise.
*   **Denoising Strength:** Controls how much the original image is preserved.
    *   `0.0`: No change.
    *   `0.3`: Slight variation (good for enhancing).
    *   `0.7`: Major changes but keeps composition.
    *   `1.0`: Completely new image (essentially Text-to-Image).

### Inpainting
Img2Img applied only to a specific area (Mask).
*   **Usage:** Fix faces, hands, remove objects, or change clothes.
*   **Masked Content:**
    *   *Original:* Keeps pixels under mask (good for slight changes).
    *   *Fill:* Blurs pixels under mask (good for replacing objects entirely).
    *   *Latent Noise:* Replaces area with noise (good for creating something from scratch).

### Outpainting
Extending the canvas beyond the original borders. The model "hallucinates" what
should be outside the frame.

### LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)
Small, add-on models (10MB - 150MB) that teach the main model a specific concept
(character, style, pose) without retraining the whole 2GB+ model.
*   **How it works:** Modifies the weights of the Cross-Attention layers in the U-Net.
*   **Trigger Words:** Most LoRAs require a specific word in the prompt to activate
    (e.g., `<lora:anime_style:1.0> mksks style`).
*   **Weight:** You can control the strength. `0.5` is subtle, `1.0` is full.

7. PROMPT ENGINEERING MASTERY
================================================================================

A prompt is a spell. The order and choice of words matter.

### Structure
1.  **Subject:** What is the main focus? (e.g., "A warrior princess")
2.  **Medium:** What is it? (e.g., "Digital illustration", "Photograph", "Oil painting")
3.  **Style:** Artistic influence (e.g., "Cyberpunk", "Steampunk", "Anime")
4.  **Artist:** Specific rendering style (e.g., "by Greg Rutkowski", "by Alphonse Mucha")
5.  **Website/Source:** Quality bias (e.g., "Trending on ArtStation", "Unreal Engine 5")
6.  **Resolution:** (e.g., "8k", "4k", "Masterpiece")
7.  **Additional Details:** (e.g., "Intricate details", "Sharp focus")
8.  **Lighting:** (e.g., "Cinematic lighting", "Golden hour", "Volumetric lighting")
9.  **Color:** (e.g., "Vivid colors", "Monochrome", "Pastel palette")

### Magic Words (Quality Boosters)
Adding these often improves the result simply because they appear in high-quality training data:
*   `masterpiece, best quality, highres, highly detailed, sharp focus`
*   `HDR, 8k, 4k, canon, nikon, f/1.8` (for photos)
*   `concept art, matte painting, illustration` (for art)

### Weighting
You can emphasize words.
*   `(word)`: Increases emphasis by 1.1x.
*   `((word))`: Increases by 1.21x.
*   `(word:1.5)`: Increases by 1.5x.
*   `[word]`: Decreases emphasis.

8. MODEL TYPES & FILE FORMATS
================================================================================

### SD 1.5 vs SDXL vs SD 2.1
*   **SD 1.5:** The most popular. Huge community, thousands of LoRAs, runs on
    lower-end hardware (4GB VRAM). Resolution: 512x512.
*   **SDXL (SD XL 1.0):** Newer, better quality, better text handling. Requires
    more VRAM (8GB+ recommended). Resolution: 1024x1024. Not recommended for iGPUs
    unless optimized.
*   **SD 2.1:** Less popular. Harder to prompt. Good for landscapes.

### File Formats
*   **SafeTensors (.safetensors):** The modern standard. Safe (cannot execute malicious
    code), fast to load. **ALWAYS use this if available.**
*   **CheckPoint (.ckpt):** The old standard. Technically a Python pickle file,
    which carries a security risk (could run arbitrary code). Avoid if possible.
*   **Diffusers Directory:** A folder containing `unet`, `vae`, `text_encoder`
    subfolders. Used by the Diffusers library and this tool internally.

### Pruned vs Full
*   **Pruned (fp16):** Weights removed that are needed for training but not generation.
    Smaller size (2GB vs 7GB). Use this for generating.
*   **EMA-only:** Even smaller, contains only the Exponential Moving Average weights.
    Perfect for inference.

9. TROUBLESHOOTING & FAQ
================================================================================

**Q: My images are black or green?**
A: This is usually a precision issue. The VAE might be overflowing in FP16.
   Try using a different VAE or switching to FP32 (slower).

**Q: Faces look distorted?**
A: SD 1.5 struggles with faces at a distance.
   1. Use a higher resolution (hires fix).
   2. Use the "Restore Faces" feature (GFPGAN/CodeFormer - external tools).
   3. Use Inpainting to regenerate just the face at a higher resolution.

**Q: Hands have 6 fingers?**
A: The classic AI problem. Hands are small and complex.
   1. Put hands in pockets prompt: `hands in pockets`.
   2. Use Negative Prompt: `bad hands, missing fingers, extra digits`.
   3. Use ControlNet (Depth or OpenPose) to force hand structure.

**Q: OpenVINO conversion takes forever!**
A: It only happens once per model/resolution. Be patient. It can take 2-5 minutes
   on a CPU. Subsequent runs are instant.

**Q: "Out of Memory" (OOM)?**
A: 1. Decrease Batch Size to 1.
   2. Decrease Resolution (Width/Height).
   3. Close other apps (Chrome uses a lot of RAM).

10. GLOSSARY
================================================================================

*   **Artifacts:** Glitches or weird pixels in the generated image.
*   **Checkpoint:** A fully trained model file.
*   **CLIP:** Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training. The text understander.
*   **Convergence:** When the image stops changing significantly with more steps.
*   **Diffusion:** The process of turning noise into data.
*   **Embedding (Textual Inversion):** A tiny file creating a new "word" for the model.
*   **Epoch:** A full cycle of training through the dataset.
*   **Gradio/Streamlit:** Web interfaces for Python apps.
*   **Hugging Face:** The "GitHub of AI". Hosts models and datasets.
*   **Inference:** The act of using the model to generate (not training).
*   **Latents:** The compressed image data.
*   **Model:** The AI brain (neural network).
*   **Noise Schedule:** How much noise is removed at each step.
*   **Overfitting:** When a model memorizes training data instead of learning concepts.
*   **Parameters:** The settings you tweak (steps, cfg, etc.).
*   **Seed:** The random number initializer.
*   **Token:** A chunk of text (word or part of word) the AI reads.
*   **Weights:** The learned numbers inside the model file.
